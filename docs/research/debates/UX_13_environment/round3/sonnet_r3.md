9. **Gemini's "equal rectangles imply the system has not decided"** as canonical one-line justification. Print verbatim in master.

## Open disputes I want documented in the master

**Dispute 1 — Three rooms vs persistent slots.**
Three-vs-one in favor of room-grammar (Sonnet/Codex/Gemini converged) over Opus's slot-grammar. Opus's R2 hardened to "persistent slot NAMES with mode-varying sizes," which is a defensible synthesis. **Recommendation:** three rooms as primary; Opus's slot *names* (HERO / SUBS / WATCHLIST / MARKET / GUTTER) as the within-room vocabulary. Fixed verb-anchor point bridges both.

**Dispute 2 — Halo permitted in Solo only vs banned outright.**
Codex's argument is strong: "static radial gradient sounds restrained in prose but becomes a halo in production." Implementation drift is the real risk. **Recommendation:** include in Solo only at 4% amber with strict anti-pattern guard (never animate, never exceed 4%, never state-map color, never attach to hover, never appear around subordinates). If master rejects, gravity in Solo falls on verb-glyph + hero-crosses-40vh + zone-level wash. Document Codex's dissent.

**Dispute 3 — Verb-glyph at 36/48pt vs 11px verb pill.**
1-vs-3. Opus's attack is partially right and partially mischaracterizes: the UX-12 verb pill is a label-of-state *inside* the ConvictionTile schema. The UX-13 verb-glyph is a composition-level focal anchor *outside* the tile. They are different objects at different layers. **Recommendation:** include verb-glyph because no other model offered a stronger non-glow gravity instrument and the brief explicitly demands "ONE opportunity should visually dominate." Document as 1-vs-3 with my position prevailing on functional grounds. Fallback if rejected: Codex's hero-crosses-40vh as sole gravity instrument.

**Dispute 4 — Hero crosses 40vh vs hero stays above cut.**
My R2 attack: cross-cut is viewport-fragile (works at 1440×900, breaks at 1280×720 or 1440×1080). **Recommendation:** if adopted, lock crossing as percentage of hero envelope (~25% of hero height), not pixel-fixed offset. Survives viewport variation. Otherwise fall back to "verb-glyph crosses cut" — the eye crosses the horizon by reading the verb just below it, the object stays above.

**Dispute 5 — Five Codex modes vs three rooms.** Resolved via Codex's R2 concession to three. Note Codex's "Pressure" variant for future iteration if bear-case urgency needs distinct treatment.

**Dispute 6 — Symmetric `Field` vs staggered `Field`.** Resolved via R3 concession above.

## Final answers per deliverable

### D1. Visual philosophy evolution
The page is the AI's editorial decision rendered as composition. Three named rooms, posture-sentence-named, never animated, never reflowed during read. Manifesto: composition is content; emptiness is a brushstroke; three rooms not infinite layouts; fold is a horizon; living = chosen.

### D2. Homepage recomposition
12-col grid, 80px outer margin, 32px gutter. Hero left-anchored cols 1–7 (Duet) or cols 1–9 (Solo). 3-col negative well right (cols 10–12) holding posture, page-state, ambient timestamp, and "since you left" delta. Subordinates below 40vh in cols 1–4 / 5–8 / 9–12. Verb-anchor fixed across rooms.

### D3. Hierarchy system
Five-step eye flow: posture sentence → verb-glyph (mass) → hero schema rows → negative well (exhale) → horizon scroll. Codex's measurable gravity rules adopted: hero envelope 2.0–2.5× standard tile area; ≥304px negative-space corridor adjacent to hero; max two investment objects share top edge.

Three rooms chosen at session-load. Layout locked for the session; explicit refresh recomputes with 340ms orchestration. Drawer open/close preserves positions. Room name lives in posture sentence's first three words. Rough split: ~15% Solo, ~60% Duet, ~25% Field.
